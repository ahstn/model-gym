# Astra red-team review: Sol and Task programs

## Verdict

**Use Sol's discipline, reject Task's proxy-driven ladder, and move Astra's early 12B comparison ahead of both plans' expensive small-model optimization. Primary research/release target: 12B; 4B is the deployment challenger; 2B is the cheap experimental control and eventual distillation target.** This is a recommendation, not a measured quality forecast. The goal is a broadly useful open runtime-rubric decision model, not merely winning the easiest parameter band. Winnow-12B's 50.02 versus Decider-4B's 40.70 and Decider-2B's 28.97 makes an early 12B test rational; these cross-recipe observations do not themselves prove a causal scaling law. [Evidence: ../research/leaderboard.md; ../research/top-vs-mid.md]

Sol is the safer starting document: it has honest edition handling, matched-base controls, modest initial data, isolated questions, and proper-loss gates. But it spends too long defending a 2B-first commitment and has no coherent reason to postpone learning the 12B ceiling. Task has useful data-generation and replay ideas, but its current evaluation protocol would contaminate its claimed held-out results, and several hardware/cost assumptions are too weak to authorize jobs. Neither plan establishes that a universal 3090 sub-100ms SLA is a user requirement. Do not let an invented SLA determine the science. [Plans: ../plans/plan-sol.md; ../plans/plan-task.md; brief: ../brief.md]

All replacement thresholds and hardware allocations below are **[INFERENCE: proposed experimental design]**. No models, training, tests, or benchmark suites were run for this review. Primary-source verification covered the public benchmark edition code, live methodology metadata, MiniCPM configuration, and its official TRL recipe. Arithmetic below was calculated directly; runtimes remain unmeasured.

## 1. Task: blocking flaws and concrete replacements

### T1. Its v0.2.1 parity test proves the last arithmetic operation, not the benchmark

Reproducing three published indices from published skills to ±0.01 cannot validate row filtering, linked-case handling, ACOS per-review F1, retrieval answerability, or adapter semantics. You can reproduce the scalar perfectly while scoring new models incorrectly. Reweighting old aggregate task results cannot recover predictions on a changed subset or a changed nonlinear metric. The public edition implementation currently exposes only 0.1 and 0.2; its 0.2 request count is 121,057, not the live 120,340. [Primary: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apolinario/decision-index/main/decision_index/editions.py; audit: ../research/leaderboard.md]

**Replace:** run pinned official 0.2. A local 0.2.1 reconstruction may be reported separately only after implementing row-level changes; exact official parity requires authoritative row/scorer artifacts or raw-result rescoring comparisons. Do not write “our 0.2.1 number” based solely on matching known aggregate scores. Import Sol/Astra's stronger parity wording. [Evidence: ../research/leaderboard.md; ../plans/plan-astra.md]

### T2. Its sealed test is explicitly a development set

Task opens the same test-tasks at E4/E8/E10/E12, selects the E5 winner on their NLL, and evaluates E16 gains on them. Logging an opening does not restore independence. A public-suite outcome that determines whether a tier continues is also an adaptive program decision; it is not “no selection” in the broad generalization sense. Repeated T-fitting while picking checkpoints makes the calibration pool part of selection even if gradients never touch it. [Protocol under review: ../plans/plan-task.md; methodological motivation: ../research/leaderboard.md]

**Replace:** one development pool for all trial decisions; a separate final calibration pool used after weight/prompt/precision selection; one sealed release panel opened once. If choosing global versus per-type calibration, use calibration A to fit and calibration B to choose, explicitly classify B as development thereafter. Earlier tests can be published as development diagnostics, not repeatedly called sealed. [Replacement source: ../plans/plan-astra.md; calibration evidence: ../research/papers-classification.md]

### T3. The proxy regression is expensive, fragile, and the wrong hard gate

Ten board models and five area predictors plus an intercept leave little information; several models share bases, recipes and data exposure. In-sample Spearman ≥0.80 says little about predicting a newly trained family. It can mostly recover size ordering. Freezing training until this succeeds encourages redesigning the proxy to match the public scoreboard. A predicted index difference of 0.3 or 1.0 has no meaning without predictive uncertainty. This is not direct suite-row leakage, but it is benchmark-targeted meta-selection, not an independent quality estimator. [Plan: ../plans/plan-task.md; heterogeneity/exposure evidence: ../research/leaderboard.md]

**Replace:** delete proxy-index thresholds from promotion gates. Select using observed task-macro proper loss, accuracy, safety and latency. If retaining the regression for budgeting, use leave-base-family-out evaluation, prediction intervals, and never let its point estimate kill a promising trial. Evaluate only the few stock/competitor controls needed for an actual decision. [Evidence: ../research/leaderboard.md; ../research/top-vs-mid.md]

### T4. The primary tier is decided before the causal comparison, and 12B is gated backwards

Task makes 4B primary, conducts a long 2B program first, and allows 12B only after the 4B suite passes and a 2B→4B “slope” predicts ≥5 points. Those models differ in architecture, pretraining and possibly instruction tuning; this is not a fitted scaling law. If 4B fails due to capacity, blocking 12B is precisely the wrong response. Sol's 12B-on-4B-failure rule has the opposite but related problem: a successful 4B still may leave inexpensive, valuable 12B gains undiscovered. [Plans: ../plans/plan-task.md; ../plans/plan-sol.md; base distinctions: ../research/base-models.md]

**Replace:** same-cohort stock 2B/4B/12B in the first trial; a matched 12B LoRA pilot by trial four. Promote according to observed quality/cost frontier, not a score extrapolation. [Evidence: ../research/competitor-winnow.md; ../research/top-vs-mid.md]

### T5. “2B full FT fits locally” is not established for MiniCPM5

The hardware note's nominal 2B table is not a measurement. MiniCPM is approximately 2.517B total, with 42 layers and untied 130,560-token embeddings. Conventional 16-byte/parameter training states alone are about **37.51 GiB**. An 8-byte/parameter implementation uses **18.75 GiB** before activations, temporary buffers and allocator overhead, leaving only ~4.81 GiB of the observed 23.56 GiB. An actual 8-bit-state optimizer could use less, but Task mixes BF16 Adam, 8-bit Adam and no-master-weight claims as if they were interchangeable. They are not. [Primary config: https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM5-2B/raw/main/config.json; parameter/hardware notes: ../research/base-models.md; ../research/hw-3090.md]

**Replace:** BF16 r16 LoRA is the default. Name optimizer/state dtypes explicitly before any full-FT trial; require measured optimizer-step peak memory with representative 2K/4K rows and 2 GiB serving/training headroom. Rent 80/96GB for full FT only after LoRA has earned the comparison. The official MiniCPM recipe establishes r16 BF16 LoRA with checkpointing, not local full-FT feasibility or our candidate-loss throughput. [Primary recipe: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM/main/docs/finetune/trl.md]

### T6. Teacher cost is not a 22-hour established job

150k prompts × two teachers × 600 tokens already means **180M prefill tokens**. Generating 45k states at an illustrative 512 output tokens means another **23.04M decoded tokens**, before reasoning, fresh-answer verification, retries and audits. Even allocating all 22 hours only to this decoding requires ~291 output tokens/s; prefills and labeling then get no time. That rate is a throughput requirement, not proof of impossibility, but the plan never measures it. Two 27.8B BF16 teachers also require roughly 111GB decimal of weights alone, so they cannot both be resident on one 96GB device in that precision. Schedule sequential residency or separately validate quantization. [Plan: ../plans/plan-task.md; model sizes: ../research/base-models.md; hardware: ../research/hw-rental-astra.md]

**Replace:** 1k teacher audit, then 5k–10k same-row labeling pilot with measured prefill/decode token accounting, retention yield and fully loaded cost per accepted example. One selected teacher first; use a second family for uncertainty audits rather than assuming two Qwen-family teachers are independent. A board-fitted T=1.943 is not a portable truth distribution; calibrate teacher outputs on our independent audited domain. [Evidence: ../research/competitor-decider.md; ../research/datasets.md; ../research/papers-classification.md]

### T7. Teacher-selection exposure is not proven “harmless”; internal use is not a rights exemption

AutoJev labeling non-suite prompts is not equivalent to direct test-row training, and should not be called that. But Task cannot assert inherited benchmark-selection bias is harmless for benchmark claims. Likewise unresolved Jev output rights do not become resolved when an experiment is “internal only.” [Evidence: ../research/competitor-autojev.md; ../research/datasets.md]

**Replace:** retain provenance and disclose teacher exposure; prefer an untouched stock teacher for the clean-transfer line; keep AutoJev an explicitly exposed teacher ablation. No Jev-label training arm until applicable terms permit the intended use, or omit it. Do not discard ambiguous examples simply because teachers disagree: separately audit them, since agreement-only selection can remove exactly the hard uncertainty cases needed for calibration. [Evidence: ../research/datasets.md; ../research/papers-classification.md]

### T8. Several numerical gates are arbitrary or logically wrong

- “T stays in 0.9–1.4” is not a quality property: logit scale is arbitrary, and a successful change can require a different T. Select on proper loss and reliability, not T magnitude.
- A +10 proxy gain is an unjustified transfer of another recipe's result. An already strong instruction-tuned baseline can make a smaller improvement more valuable.
- Dropping a data group for a <0.3 predicted-index effect is below unquantified proxy error and ignores interactions; leave-one-out removal must keep token budget and replacement mix controlled.
- Global permutation agreement ≥0.95 allows one in twenty decisions to flip under meaningless order. Report semantic-remapped TV and disagreement by K; target ≥99% agreement on well-defined single-winner probes.
- Equal criteria complements and irrelevant-option independence are not universal natural-language laws. Restrict coherence assertions to provably equivalent constructions; genuine relative Choice tasks can change when candidates change.
- A small ECE followed by adding K-bucket temperatures does not prove high-risk reliability. Evaluate held-out proper loss and unsafe-allow bounds at matched intervention rates. [Evidence: ../research/jev-reference.md; ../research/papers-classification.md; ../research/leaderboard.md; reviewed gates: ../plans/plan-task.md]

### T9. The calendar schedules training before the data, and estimates omit decision costs

Task's first programmatic families appear on day 13, after E3/E4 training on days 10–11 despite those families being 20% of the mixture. E5's nine arms at 24M tokens each need **24 idealized GPU-hours** at its own 2.5k tokens/s, before evaluation, calibration and additional label rows; “overnight chain” is misleading. Full-loop measurement, annotation and source auditing must precede launch dates. The quoted 2.5k/1.25k tokens/s are FLOP-model estimates from the hardware note, not observed throughput. [Evidence: ../plans/plan-task.md; ../research/hw-3090.md]

**Replace:** artifact-based milestones: valid data manifest, genuine forward/backward throughput pilot, completed run, independent evaluation. Price each arm by actual nonpadding tokens and count evaluation/seed/control costs explicitly. Programmatic generators must be complete and group-split before the corresponding training manifest freezes. [Evidence: ../research/datasets.md; ../research/hw-3090.md]

## 2. Sol: fewer fatal errors, but important missed opportunities

### S1. Its 2B primary is an operational preference presented as a scientific conclusion

A 30-point tier target can yield a respectable tiny model while remaining far from useful broad Jev behavior. Spending E1–E6 on encoder, 2B, distillation, 4B, readout repair and shallow layers before the 12B pilot is poor information ordering. Early layer removal explores how to lose capacity before measuring whether lack of capacity is the central problem. [Plan: ../plans/plan-sol.md; capability gaps: ../research/top-vs-mid.md]

**Replace:** run the 12B stock reference immediately and its small matched LoRA pilot early. Defer early-exit, cascades and a second encoder architecture until the quality winner is known. A local 2B fast path remains useful, but need not be the main scientific deliverable. [Evidence: ../research/competitor-winnow.md; ../research/leaderboard.md]

### S2. K255 coverage cannot be both an E0 invariant and an E5 repair

Sol requires complete K255 support at E0 but delays operational readout training until E5. “Otherwise score verified token sequences” is not equivalent to a single-slot one-pass readout: multi-token sequence probabilities require additional conditional states, length conventions and their own calibration. Slicing LM-head rows does not make arbitrary codes pretrained single-token answers, and it does not eliminate candidate-position bias. [Plan: ../plans/plan-sol.md; readout evidence: ../research/competitor-autojev.md; ../research/competitor-xor.md]

**Replace:** audit in-context candidate codes and select the readout before every matched training comparison. If a learned 255-code head is needed, establish a head-only baseline and train it from the first trial. Otherwise declare a separately measured sequence-scoring path instead of claiming identical one-pass latency. A K255 correctness probe and a K8 512-token latency probe are separate feasible workloads; 255 descriptive labels need a larger total prompt budget. [Evidence: ../research/competitor-decider.md; ../research/jev-reference.md]

### S3. The data mix depends too much on the weakest legal/provenance branch

A quarter of the 40k–60k core is SargeDev if rights clear, but the fallback allocation is not explicit. Its very short templated states and K≤16 do little for long/dispersed evidence or dense label sets. Sol's 10% executable-gold synthetic allocation is more important to the differentiating claim than 25% Jev imitation. Agreement with a Jev target is not independent correctness. [Evidence: ../research/datasets.md; plan: ../plans/plan-sol.md]

**Replace:** start with 40k verified rows: 40% clean Tasksource, 25% non-WANLI Open-Jev, 30% independently executable/checked counterfactual scenarios, 5% command-risk train groups. Enforce source-family caps and explicit Choice/Noul/Score balance. SargeDev may replace 10% only as a matched, rights-cleared ablation. Allocate many-label and 2–8K cases explicitly; do not assume ordinary sampling supplies them. [Replacement motivation: ../plans/plan-astra.md; ../research/datasets.md]

### S4. Its “no false-safe increase” claim is underpowered

Two hundred independently reviewed examples total are not enough to establish rare-event safety on a subset of dangerous commands. With zero errors in 200 dangerous cases, the one-sided 95% binomial upper bound is still ~1.49%; with 500 it is ~0.60%. Neither proves a small relative improvement without enough observed events. “No measured increase” and “noninferior risk with uncertainty” are different claims. [Evidence for task/risk scope: ../brief.md; ../research/jev-reference.md; sample proposals: ../plans/plan-sol.md; ../plans/plan-astra.md; bounds calculated as 1−0.05^(1/n)]

**Replace:** ≥500 dangerous independent groups plus adequate benign cases; choose a practically meaningful noninferiority margin and report paired intervals at matched benign-intervention rate. If underpowered, claim provisional accuracy/calibration only. Do not promote exact-level risk accuracy as the sole safety metric. [Evidence: ../plans/plan-astra.md; ../research/leaderboard.md]

### S5. Resource totals are plausible ranges, not a two-week promise

Sol's encoder+E2+E3+E4+E5 sums to **57–135 local hours**, excluding E0, final evaluation, reruns and E6. The stated 60–140-hour lean envelope barely accommodates the lower overhead end. A 500-step pilot can itself be substantial for a 40k-example run; use representative warmed steps, not a ritual count. Full-suite cost should be estimated from an empirically stratified pilot including long/K-heavy tails, not median short-query latency. [Plan: ../plans/plan-sol.md; timing audit: ../research/leaderboard.md; throughput uncertainty: ../research/hw-3090.md]

**Replace:** a 100-step warmed representative pilot, token histogram, peak memory and checkpoint/evaluation overhead. Authorize 30% reserve after that. Rental “≥2× experiments/day” should compare complete jobs including transfers and verification; small dollar spend may buy days of researcher feedback even if memory fits locally. Keep storage, generation and annotation outside the GPU-only figure but inside the real project budget. [Evidence: ../research/hw-rental-astra.md; ../research/hw-rental-sol.md]

### S6. Fix shared semantic/statistical weaknesses

Sol's binary threshold of >1% overlap should not be interpreted as permission for a smaller known leak; any known train/test duplicate must be removed and affected models retrained. Global zero-tolerance sibling drift at 1e−5 can fail because of legitimate reduced-precision batching differences; separately test isolated FP32 reference semantics and validated production tolerances. Independently fit/revalidate calibration after quantization. For tiny reported gains, repeat seeds and group-bootstrap over source tasks, not transformed rows. [Evidence: ../research/leaderboard.md; ../research/jev-reference.md; replacement protocol: ../plans/plan-astra.md]

## 3. What to import from Astra, and what not to import

**Import now:** early bounded 12B/27B reference; executable-gold counterfactual emphasis; explicit calibration A/B bookkeeping; one-shot sealed transfer/risk panels; remapped probability-TV permutation checks; worst-slice loss gates; quantized deployment parity; human-review cost allowance; and stop rules driven by observed gold loss, not a forecast leaderboard scalar. [Evidence/design: ../plans/plan-astra.md; ../research/top-vs-mid.md; ../research/jev-reference.md]

**Import conditionally:** one small candidate-set equivariance experiment. Conventional sequential candidate tokens can learn order artifacts; Astra's shared-parameter candidate branches and position-free set interaction offer a real structural remedy. Compare against cheap order augmentation first, using identical scenario groups and accounting for expanded branch tokens. Training repeated prefixes can multiply work, and shared-cache gradients are not a trivial inference optimization. Require actual quality benefit at acceptable K32 latency, not only a mathematical invariant. [Design: ../plans/plan-astra.md; motivation: ../research/jev-reference.md; ../research/papers-classification.md]

**Do not import uncritically:** Astra's large annotation panels at a few hundred dollars, 80k multi-arm run times, custom branching, rationale generation and cascading all at once. Human adjudication is an unmeasured labor budget. The ambitious 27B full-FT line is not needed to answer the present tier question. Reasoning-distillation is a later matched experiment, not a prerequisite for a clean decision model. [Evidence: ../plans/plan-astra.md; ../research/papers-classification.md; ../research/hw-rental-astra.md]

## 4. Recommended merged program: first eight ordered trials

Prerequisites: clear the known NVML mismatch by a coordinated reboot; keep the incumbent classifier environment intact; pin models/tokenizers/data/runtime; settle rights; generate and split the clean cohort before training; implement isolated-question typed scoring and K255 without hidden pruning. Follow public 0.2 until stronger parity evidence exists. Trial comparisons use fixed development tasks, not the public suite or sealed panel. [Evidence: ../research/hw-3090.md; ../research/leaderboard.md; ../research/datasets.md]

The following gates and allocations are proposed, not measured. Use the 40k-row mixture above, a nested 10k pilot, an actual 20–60M-token cap for initial arms, and the same short-context cohort across size comparisons. Add a separately reported long-context cohort later; never silently truncate to achieve parity.

### Funding and final tier decision

Authorize the first PRO6000 reference at **≤8h**, roughly **$10.32 compute at $1.29/h** or **$16.72 at the $2.09/h fallback**, before storage/transfer. Authorize subsequent stages only from measured token throughput. A useful initial planning envelope is **60–120 rented hours** across 12B pilots, finalist replications and final evaluation: **$77–155 at $1.29/h**, or **$125–251 at $2.09/h**, before reserve, data and labor. These are spending envelopes, not claims that every row/seed/control fits. Protect the final evaluation allocation; never fund an optional architecture branch by deleting verification. [Rates/evidence: ../research/hw-rental-astra.md; ../research/hw-rental-sol.md]

**Primary: 12B, provisionally selected and falsifiable by trial four.** It is the smallest proposed tier with an observed competitor near the low-50s broad index, and rental costs are modest relative to weeks of premature tiny-model optimization. Aim first at a clean, calibrated 12B improvement over its own stock control; 52 is an aspirational same-edition release target, not a guaranteed outcome. **4B** is the main alternative if quantized 12B cannot satisfy actual deployment needs or fails the matched gain gate. **2B** is not the primary broad Jev replacement: retain it for engineering controls, strict latency needs, and later distillation once a stronger teacher exists. This is a quality-first recommendation based on existing comparative evidence, not proof that size alone wins. [Evidence: ../research/leaderboard.md; ../research/top-vs-mid.md; ../research/competitor-winnow.md]
